# Barry Hua’s portfolio

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Recruiters and engineers evaluating Barry for software engineering roles. They need to identify his contribution, inspect a working example, and find a way to follow up.

## Product Purpose

Introduce Barry as a person with a distinct visual taste, then let visitors explore the software he builds and the ideas behind it. His projects provide a natural place to discover his applied mathematics background. A successful visit gives someone enough evidence to explain one thing Barry built, how it works, and what he contributed.

## Brand Commitments

The chosen theme is an explorable pixel garden, with a sakura tree at its center. Barry wants visitors to pause because the site looks beautiful and makes them curious about the person who made it. The first screen should feel alive. How extensively the pixel treatment carries into the rest of the site remains open.

## Evidence on Hand

- Atlas demonstrates itinerary planning and routing. The existing demo is available at `/demos/atlas/`.
- LoL Ping has a demo at `/demos/lol-ping/`, a screenshot, and a linked source repository.
- The Fire and Ice project links to the team repository. Barry’s description identifies his work on movement, collisions, and controls.
- The portfolio includes experience, a waterfront portrait, and GitHub, LinkedIn, and email links.

## Product Principles

- Give visitors a direct route from the first screen to Barry’s work, including when they skip the tree interaction.
- Describe Barry’s contribution to each project and pair it with the strongest available evidence.
- Carry the existing keyboard-accessible path to the main content through the rebuild.

## Garden lighting

Auto is the default. The garden follows each visitor’s local time and gradually changes between spring afternoon, late sunset, and blue hour while the page is open. A compact time control lets visitors preview any of the three or return to Auto. Landmarks stay in the same places across lighting changes.

## Project workshop

Three handpicked projects sit on the desk. The bookshelf contains every project, including those three. Selecting the shelf opens a close-up with readable titles. A visitor can select a project directly on the desk or select its book from the shelf. Adding a project to the shelf does not require a new desk object.

Both paths open the same pixel-art book for that project. The open book shows the project title and two actions, “Try demo” and “View on GitHub,” with a project picture beneath them. Both action slots stay visible. If a demo or repository link is unavailable, its slot displays “Coming soon” as a non-interactive placeholder until the link is ready.

## About album

A teal photo album labeled “About me” rests on the bench by the pond. Selecting it opens a spread with Barry’s waterfront portrait on the left and a short personal introduction on the right. Visitors can return directly to the garden. The introduction in the mockup is sample copy.

## Experience noticeboard

A wooden noticeboard beside the Projects hut opens to one pinned note per role. Selecting a note reveals Barry’s role and contributions.

## Contact mailbox

A small red mailbox beside the garden path opens a postcard with Email, LinkedIn, and GitHub links. The Contact navigation link opens the same postcard directly.

## Landmark labels

On first arrival, all four clickable landmarks show their names on wooden plaques: Projects, Experience, About me, and Contact. Selecting “Hide labels” hides the lettering and keeps each wooden backing visible. Selecting “Show labels” restores the names. The landmarks remain interactive in both states.
